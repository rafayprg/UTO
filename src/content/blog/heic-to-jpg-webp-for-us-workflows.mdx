An online portal asks for a standard JPG, but the photograph snapped on your iPhone ends with `.heic`. Simply renaming the file extension from `IMG_1042.heic` to `IMG_1042.jpg` does not change the underlying data: the container still holds an Apple High Efficiency Image File (HEIF) bitstream encoded with HEVC. When uploaded, the portal’s backend server rejects the payload with an "Unsupported file format" or "Corrupt image" error.

To resolve this, you need to decode the compressed video-frame pixels and re-encode them into an industry-standard image container that the destination understands. The [HEIC to JPG / WebP converter](/tools/image/heic-converter/) performs this entire decoding and re-encoding process locally inside your browser, leaving your original photo untouched on your camera roll.

## Choose JPG for compatibility, WebP for modern web performance

Understanding your target destination dictates which output format you should choose:

| Feature Dimension | Apple HEIC (Source) | Standard JPG / JPEG | Modern WebP |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Browser Compatibility** | Apple Safari & iOS only | 100% universal (all devices) | 98%+ modern browsers |
| **Compression Efficiency** | Exceptional (50% smaller than JPG) | Baseline standard | ~30% smaller than JPG |
| **Alpha Transparency** | Limited alpha channel support | No transparency (solid white) | Full 8-bit alpha transparency |
| **Portal Acceptance** | Poor (rejected by most forms) | Universal (DMV, IRS, banks) | High on modern CMS & web apps |
| **Metadata (EXIF GPS)** | Embedded by camera | Stripped on canvas export | Stripped on canvas export |

- **Choose JPG** when submitting receipts to payroll, attaching documents to insurance claims, uploading state IDs to government portals, or sending attachments to recipients on older Windows machines.
- **Choose WebP** when preparing images for your blog, Next.js or WordPress website, or web applications where Google Core Web Vitals and fast page load speeds matter most.

## Dialing in the optimal quality slider value

Encoder quality is an algorithmic parameter, not a linear percentage of file size. Lowering the slider from 90% to 75% does not mean the output will be 15% smaller—it adjusts discrete cosine transform (DCT) quantization tables.

To find the optimal balance for strict upload portals (such as a 2 MB portal ceiling):
- **90% to 95% Quality:** Recommended for formal document scans, receipts with fine print, contract signatures, and architectural photography.
- **75% to 85% Quality:** The gold standard for web publishing, product catalogs, and everyday social attachments. It typically cuts file size in half compared to 95% with zero perceptible distortion.
- **50% to 65% Quality:** Use only when dealing with extreme upload constraints (e.g., portals limited to under 500 KB). Small text may exhibit slight ringing artifacts around high-contrast edges.

## Automatic privacy protection via metadata stripping

When you take a photo with an iPhone, the device automatically records extensive EXIF data:
- Exact GPS latitude, longitude, and elevation
- Camera serial number and lens metadata
- Timestamp, exposure values, and focal distance
- Facial recognition bounding markers

When Localtools decodes your HEIC image onto the browser Canvas and exports it as JPG or WebP, **all EXIF geolocation metadata is automatically stripped**. The exported file contains pure visual pixel data, protecting your home address and personal whereabouts before you post pictures to public message boards, forums, or classified ads.
