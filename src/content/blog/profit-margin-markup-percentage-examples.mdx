An item costs $60 to manufacture and retails for $100. The raw dollar profit is $40. Its gross profit margin is **40%**, but its markup is **66.67%**.

Both numbers are mathematically correct, yet confusing margin with markup in an ecommerce pricing spreadsheet can quickly lead to underpricing inventory, miscalculating marketing budgets, and operating at an accidental loss.

## The Core Formulas: Margin vs Markup vs Change

The fundamental distinction lies in which value occupies the denominator:

| Metric | Business Definition | Mathematical Formula | Worked Example ($60 Cost, $100 Price) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gross Profit** | Raw dollar earnings | `Revenue - COGS` | `$100 - $60 = $40` |
| **Profit Margin** | Percentage of revenue retained as profit | `(Profit / Selling Price) * 100` | `($40 / $100) * 100 = 40.0%` |
| **Markup Percentage** | Percentage added on top of cost | `(Profit / Cost) * 100` | `($40 / $60) * 100 = 66.67%` |
| **Target Retail Price** | Price required to hit target margin | `Cost / (1 - Target Margin)` | `$60 / (1 - 0.40) = $100.00` |

## The Four Calculation Modes Explained

### 1. Percentage of an Amount
Answers: *"What dollar discount does a 20% coupon represent on a $150 shopping cart?"*
- **Formula:** `Result = (Percentage / 100) * Base`
- Enter `20` in Percentage and `150` in Amount.
- Result: **$30.00**.
- Final Price: `$150 - $30 = $120.00`.

### 2. What Percentage?
Answers: *"If 45 out of 180 customer survey respondents purchased an upsell, what is the conversion rate?"*
- **Formula:** `Share = (Part / Whole) * 100`
- Enter `45` in Part and `180` in Whole.
- Result: **25.0%**.

### 3. Percentage Change (Increases and Decreases)
Answers: *"Our website traffic rose from 80 daily signups to 100 daily signups. What is our growth rate?"*
- **Formula:** `Change = ((New - Original) / Original) * 100`
- Enter `80` in Original and `100` in New.
- Result: **+25.0% Increase**.

#### The Reversibility Pitfall:
Notice what happens if traffic drops back from 100 to 80:
- Original: `100`, New: `80`.
- Result: **-20.0% Decrease**.

A 25% increase followed by a 25% decrease does **not** return you to baseline. Starting with $100, increasing by 25% yields $125; decreasing $125 by 25% drops you to $93.75.

### 4. Profit Margin and Markup
Answers: *"We source a product for $60 and want a 35% margin after shipping. What price must we charge?"*

Many operators make the mistake of calculating:
`$60 + (35% of $60) = $60 + $21 = $81 (INCORRECT)`

If you sell at $81, your actual margin is:
`($81 - $60) / $81 = $21 / $81 = 25.9%`

You fell nearly 10 percentage points below your targeted profit.

To guarantee a true 35% margin, you must divide by the reciprocal:
```text
Target Retail Price = Cost / (1 - Desired Margin)
                    = $60 / (1 - 0.35)
                    = $60 / 0.65
                    = $92.31
```

## Percentage Points vs Relative Percentage Change

When reporting financial metrics, distinguish between **percentage points (pp)** and **percentage change (%)**:
- If an ecommerce checkout abandonment rate drops from **10%** down to **8%**:
  - The rate decreased by **2 percentage points** (`10 - 8 = 2`).
  - The relative reduction is **20%** (`(2 / 10) * 100`).

Conflating these terms can mislead executive stakeholders and misrepresent campaign ROI.
